## Abstract

**Abstract:** This paper demonstrates that physical singularities—states of infinite density or zero spatial extent—are mathematical artifacts resulting from the misapplication of asymptotic limits and the division-by-zero fallacy. By applying the principles of Inertial Relativity, we show that every physical system must maintain finite mass and spatial extent. We re-evaluate key "singular" scenarios, including black holes and the cosmic origin, as asymptotic approaches rather than reachable states, providing a consistent framework that eliminates mathematical blow-ups from physical models.

## Introduction: The Myth of the Point-Origin

The concept of the physical singularity—a state where density becomes infinite and spatial extent vanishes—represents one of the most significant internal obstructions in the history of science. Historically, the notion of a singularity emerged not from physical observation, but as a mathematical artifact that was eventually "marketed" into mainstream physics to support specific cosmological models.

As the Lambda CDM model (The Big Bang) sought to explain cosmic redshift through spatial expansion, it required a "point-origin" to satisfy its initial conditions. However, because mathematics does not support the reaching of a limit at zero, a systematic campaign was established to redefine physical "reality" to fit the theory. This campaign, which I contend amounts to a coordinated evasion of scientific rigor, forced an entire generation of physicists to treat mathematical blow-ups as physical systems.

The implications of this misconception are profound. For 75 years, theoretical exploration has been restricted by the presumption that these "singular" states are reachable facts rather than proofs of model failure. We have built entire cosmological frameworks on the premise that an approach to zero is equivalent to becoming zero—a logical fallacy that violates the very definition of a mathematical limit. 

In this paper, I re-evaluate these singular systems through the lens of Inertial Relativity, demonstrating that for every finite physical input, there must exist a finite physical output. We transcend the Planck scale not by switching to a quantum universe, but by recognizing that the Planck scale marks a limit of observability, not a fundamental change in the laws of classical physics.

## The Mathematics of Physical Reachability

### The Asymptotic Limit: $1/x \to 0$

The most fundamental error in singularity physics is the misinterpretation of the limit. In mathematics, as $x \to \infty$, the expression $1/x$ approaches $0$. The definition of a limit is unambiguous: it is a boundary that the function *never* reaches or exceeds. To conclude that a physical system "reaches" a state of zero volume or infinite density is to state that the limit has been surpassed—an impossibility by definition.

1. **Insignificance $\neq$ Non-existence**: While a value may become negligible relative to a local scale, it never becomes zero. 
2. **The "Approaching" Fallacy**: An approach that continues forever (as $x$ approaches infinity) describes a persistent state of existence, not a transition into non-existence.
3. **Finite Inputs, Finite Outputs**: In any consistent physical model, a finite amount of mass distributed over any spatial extent will result in a finite density. The "blow-up" only occurs when one artificially inserts a zero into the denominator.

### Floating Point Reality and Division by Zero

In both theoretical and computational physics, the concept of a "literal zero" is problematic. Because any physical ratio calculation represents a form of floating-point math, there is never a possibility for the literal absence of value required for a true division by zero.

- **Out of Bounds**: If an exponent becomes too large or too small to represent, it is an "out of bounds" error for the observer/instrument, not a "singularity" in the system itself.
- **Fallacy of the Blow-up**: A division-by-zero "error" in a physical calculation is a symptom of a flawed premise, not a description of a physical event. If you are asking what fraction of zero a number is, you are asking an illogical question; the system you are modeling must have extent to exist.

### The Myth of the Planck Scale

The Planck scale is often cited as the boundary where classical physics fails and a "quantum transition" occurs. Under Inertial Relativity, we recognize this as an observational phenomenon related to the limits of our observational mechanisms. Size and scale are relative; there is no point where "spatial extent" fundamentally changes its nature. Gravity and inertia continue to function at all scales, and the "quantum blow-up" is merely another instance of mathematical limits being incorrectly interpreted as physical thresholds.

## Specific Examples of Model Failure

### Black Holes as Asymptotic Systems

A black hole is often described as a "singularity" hidden behind an event horizon. Under Inertial Relativity, we recognize that as mass density increases, the local inertial scale factor $k_i$ increases, leading to extreme time dilation. However, the system never reaches a state of infinite density. 

The event horizon is not a point of zero time or infinite gravity; it is simply the boundary where the escape velocity equals $c$. The matter within remains a physical system with finite dimensions and mass. To describe it as a singularity is to confuse a limit of light escape with a physical collapse into non-existence.

### The Cosmic Origin and Expansion Limits

The Big Bang singularity is a mathematical projection of observed redshift backward in time under the false premise of cosmic expansion. If one accepts that redshift is a manifestation of relative inertial time dilation between observer and observed, the need for a "point-origin" vanishes. The universe does not "diverge" from a point; rather, our observational perspective is limited by the relative scale of the systems we observe. 

### UV Catastrophes and Navier-Stokes Blow-ups

The "UV catastrophe" was a failure of the classical model of blackbody radiation, which was resolved by quantization. However, from the perspective of Inertial Relativity, it represents another instance of a model reaching its domain of applicability. Similarly, Navier-Stokes "blow-ups" in turbulent flow are not physical failures but mathematical artifacts resulting from treating a fluid as a continuum down to an infinitely small scale. In the real world, the finite extent of the constituent particles (atoms/molecules) provides the natural cutoff that prevents a true singularity.

## Observational Scenarios: The Role of the Observer

The perception of singular or divergent behavior is often a product of the observer's own inertial scale. As demonstrated in my research on the "Inertial Scale," any observation is mediated by the moment of inertia (MOI) of the observational mechanism.

1. **Observing the Macro (Galactic Clusters)**: When we peer at vast galactic clusters, we are observing systems with immensely larger moments of inertia than our own (e.g., the $\beta$-Keratin in our retinas). The resulting redshift is not evidence of expansion, but a measure of the relative pace of time in a much larger inertial system.
2. **Observing the Micro (Molecules)**: Conversely, observing molecules reveals systems with much smaller moments of inertia. The resonance of these systems (UV or blue-shifted) reflects their faster relative temporal pace. 
3. **The Scaling Consistency**: In all cases, whether observing a "black hole" or a subatomic particle, the laws of physics remain consistent. There is no preferred scale, and the "singularity" only appears when the observer ignores the finite extent of the observed system.

## Conclusion: The Persistence of Mass and Extent

The physical singularity does not exist. It is a mathematical phantom born of a failure to respect the definition of a limit and the fundamental relationship between inertia and time. All physical systems, no matter how dense or small, maintain both mass and extent. By eliminating the "blow-up" from our models, we reclaim a deterministic, classical physics that operates consistently at all scales.
