### Understanding the relationship between time and inertia

Gravity is simply the tendency for any two objects to converge upon their shared center of mass in an effort to reduce their collective moment of inertia, and thus reduce their energy footprint. Every particle in the universe shares this relationship with every other particle, but the inverse square law causes particles to attract more to more massive particles in the vicinity.

To make sense of this, one must presume that inertia (linear motion) and moment of inertia (rotational motion) defines the timescale for any system or subsystem. For spherical objects, this can be close to the same about all axes. Under this presumption, the GTD effects progress correctly as a static mass system reduces in radius. But this would also explain gravitational waves mathematically as well. a shock wave in space casues an outward propigation of compressed cosmic material. This wave increases the moment of inertia of the compressed region, resulting in a gravitational wave (a ripple in time really). The gravitational field is matter ( dust, asteroids, clouds, stars, etc.).

## Time is emergent of inertia

If time is in fact emergent of inertia for any system or subsystem about any axis or in any linear direction, this would mean the following about time:

1. Time is dimensional and both linear and rotational. This means that every system has six degrees of freedom regarding the pace of time: linear x/y/x and rotational x/y/z. A non-spherical system (like a cosmic cloud, a nebula, a galactic cluster, etc.) would have a different pace of time.
2. If time is about inertia, time is about size, shape, and density. A system that is more dense for the same spatial extent has proportionally greater time dilation relative to the reference system.
3. Time is distinct at every point in space to infinite precision.
4. Time is dynamic
5. For any system about any axis or in any direction, time is static regardless of how that system is subdivided. (i.e two semispheres, a core+shell, or 1e^23 individual molecules, etc.)
6. Time is relative and only meaningful relative to some comparator. As we already know.
7. If time is about mass distribution, then all particles must possess both mass and radius, regardless of scale or observability. This would invalidate the notion of singularity or Planck scale limitations, or the idea that there are, in fact, discrete regimes of physics that behave differently. It would point to a much more uniform universe.
8. If every system must have mass and extent, and if the pace of time is equal the moment of inertia around an axis, then the pace of time can never have a value that is less than OR EQUAL to 0. To the extent that no system can have negative extent or negative mass, time ALWAYS progresses, confirming causality.
